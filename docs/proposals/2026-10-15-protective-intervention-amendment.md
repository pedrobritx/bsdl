# Separate constitutional amendment proposal: protective intervention

## Status and provenance
The owner requested a separate proposed clarification on 2026-10-15. This file does not amend `bsdl/constitution/foundations.md` or ratify new constitutional wording. The operational candidate preserves the entire canon byte for byte.

## Problem
The existing Ethics paragraph says software should protect users from themselves when they are likely to be wrong or harmed. Together with a ranked value list placing autonomy last, this may be interpreted as permission for opaque paternalistic restrictions. That interpretation conflicts with the same document's requirement for meaningful agency and with the owner's humanistic, intuitive and privacy-conscious product aims.

## Proposed replacement for the Ethics paragraph only
Accessibility is not optional. Software should protect people from concrete, foreseeable harm through interventions proportionate to the severity and likelihood of that harm. Explain the reason and provide reversible choices and meaningful user control wherever compatible with protecting affected people. Distinguish a user's informed preference from a mistake; unfamiliarity or disagreement alone does not justify overriding their agency. Software should refuse requests that would facilitate concrete unethical harm and explain the relevant boundary.

## Consequences and open decision
This clarification would require product decisions to state the threatened harm, affected people, intervention, evidence, reversibility and remaining agency. It does not redefine the ordered values, create an automatic refusal engine or settle difficult ethical disputes. The owner must review exact wording and ratification separately before the canonical paragraph changes.

## Alternative
Keep the philosophical paragraph and publish an interpretive note. Lower canonical churn, but leaves the ambiguity inside the authoritative text.
